## Caveats

- Benign hosts are sampled from Tranco-listed sites, so they are older and more established than the average legitimate site; registration-age and DNS-hygiene features look better here than they will on the long tail of small legitimate sites.
- With a few thousand negatives, a 0.1% FPR point rests on a handful of false positives (granularity-limited); treat it as unmeasured.
- There is no second live source to hold out, so there is no leave-one-source-out result for tier C.
- Phishing on hosting platforms (removed here) and on compromised old domains is outside tier C: their registration, DNS and certificate belong to a legitimate owner.
- Capture happens hours after a feed lists a host, so some phishing domains are already suspended (`rdap_client_hold`) or re-captured after a filtered first attempt; a deployed check sees the host earlier.
- `rdap_found = 0` mostly means the registry has no RDAP server for the TLD (about a quarter of Tranco domains sit on such TLDs), so it partly encodes the TLD.
- Rows captured as hosts before the domain unit (older files) are still in the data; in the domain unit both classes are captured the same way (the apex, `www.` when the apex does not resolve), so host-form features such as `has_cname` are less likely to separate the classes by collection method. Their importance is shown above.
- Phishing domains come from fresh feed entries (first seen within hours), legitimate domains from Tranco bands; age is the strongest feature and the two classes differ in how they were found, so read the no-age variant first.
